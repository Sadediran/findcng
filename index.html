Is CNG safe?

The short answer: a certified CNG system is safe. Fake cylinders and quack installers are the danger.

Properly designed, certified and professionally installed automotive CNG systems are subject to strict safety standards and inspections. Unauthorised cylinders, uncertified conversion kits and improper installation increase the risk of serious incidents.

Of the incidents investigated so far, each one traced to uncertified equipment or unqualified installation — fake or welded cylinders, cooking-gas cylinders, air-conditioner cylinders, or overfilling.

What actually causes explosions

1. Fake or welded cylinders. A local welder fabricates a cylinder from scrap metal. It was never designed or tested to hold gas at high pressure. This caused the Benin City explosion of October 2024 — 3 people severely injured, one leg amputated. The welder was arrested. (Source: Standards Organisation of Nigeria; Punch / BusinessDay, Oct 2024.)

2. The wrong cylinder entirely. LPG cooking-gas cylinders and even air-conditioner cylinders have been fitted as CNG cylinders. They are not built for CNG pressure. One car was destroyed in September 2026 after an AC cylinder was fitted with AC pipe. (Source: Facebook video, Senior Advocate of Truth, Sep 2026.)

3. Overfilling. Filling past the cylinder's safe limit. Kaduna police preliminary findings linked the April 2026 Kaduna bus explosion to overfilling — police spokesman Mansir Hassan said on 5 April 2026 that the bus “exploded due to overfilling of its gas tank”, and the Kaduna State Government (Commissioner Ahmed Maiyaki) said the explosion was “traced to a technical fault linked to overfilling”. The operator, Silver Luxury Travel, rejected the claim; Governor Uba Sani ordered a multi-agency probe; and no final finding has been published as of 18 November 2026. Note: the police described the bus as LNG-propelled; press reports called it CNG. (Sources: Kaduna police statement, 5 Apr 2026; Kaduna State Government; Punch, “Kaduna bus explosion caused by overfilled gas, not bomb — Police”, 5 Apr 2026.)

4. Quack installers. Roadside technicians without accreditation, fitting kits to engines that are not in good condition. The Minister of State for Petroleum Resources (Gas) has blamed unverified, uncertified conversions — many by roadside technicians — directly for the wave of explosions. (Source: Minister Ekperikpe Ekpo, reported 2026.)

Myth vs fact

Myth: “CNG cars are time bombs.”
Fact: Of the incidents investigated so far, each one traced to uncertified equipment or unqualified installation — fake cylinders or quack fitting. A certified system, installed by an accredited centre, is built and inspected to strict standards.

Myth: “It will blow up in the heat.”
Fact: CNG ignites at about 700°C — petrol ignites at about 455°C. CNG needs much more heat to catch fire. And CNG is lighter than air: if it leaks, it rises and disperses instead of pooling on the ground like petrol.

Myth: “Any cylinder will do.”
Fact: Only certified automotive CNG cylinders — multi-layer steel or composite, pressure-tested. A cooking-gas cylinder or AC cylinder is not a CNG cylinder, whatever anyone tells you.

Myth: “Conversion is a one-time job.”
Fact: Cylinders must be tested every 3 years. Ask your centre when your cylinder is due for its test.

Stay safe — the checklist

✅ Convert only at an accredited centre — check it on the Centres tab first.

✅ Ask to see the accreditation certificate and the cylinder's certification markings.

✅ Never accept a welded, second-hand or cooking-gas cylinder.

✅ Make sure your engine is in good condition before converting — CNG reveals existing engine problems.

✅ Get the cylinder tested every 3 years.

⚠️ If you smell gas or hear hissing: switch off, get out of the vehicle, move away, and go to an accredited centre. Never try to fix a gas system yourself.

Safety content is drawn from NADDC / SON / Pi-CNG findings and news reporting (Oct 2024 – Nov 2026). If in doubt, go to an accredited centre — never take DIY advice on gas systems from social media.

Help & questions

How do I find gas near me?

Open the Map tab, enter your postcode or tap “Use my location”, and the nearest stations appear below the map, sorted by distance. Green means gas was reported available in the last 2 hours; red means a driver reported it finished.

How do I report gas at a station?

Open the station, tap “Report gas status here”, pick one of the four options, and add the queue time if you know it. No account needed. Your report stays live for 2 hours, then expires. If the gas comes back at a station marked finished, tap Back to normal.

What do the badges mean?

Officially accredited — matched on the Pi-CNG accredited centres register. Independently verified — registered here and passed human verification. Pending verification — submitted, awaiting review. Needs re-verification — from a 2024 list, not yet re-checked. See the Centres tab for the full explanation.

Why is a station not on the map?

Only entries with recorded coordinates appear on the map. Coordinates are never guessed — an entry without them appears in the list with its address only. If you know a station's exact spot, ask the centre to register with a GPS pin.

Postcodes — how location works here

Location works with the NIPOST postcode system: the old 6-digit area codes (e.g. 300001 for Benin City GPO) and the new 11-character digital postcodes launched 1 October 2026 (e.g. FC 02 A09 DB 09). A 6-digit code covers a whole district, so it centres the map approximately. An 11-character code identifies one building — but NIPOST does not give out building coordinates through its API, so the app asks you to set your exact spot with GPS or a map tap. Find your building's code free at postcode.gov.ng/find.

Does it work offline?

The station list and the app itself are cached on your phone, so the directory works with patchy signal. Live reports always need a connection — a stale report would mislead you, so the app never shows cached reports as live.

How do I add my centre?

Use “Add your centre” in the top menu. You will need your accreditation certificate (photo or scan, under 2 MB). Nothing goes live until a moderator verifies it — Pi-CNG list cross-check, certificate inspection, and a phone confirmation.

A listing is wrong. What do I do?

Open the station and tap “Report an incorrect listing” (or “Report a closure”). A moderator reviews it. Listing reports open once the database table is set up — until then the app will tell you plainly that they are not open yet.

Privacy

Plain-language privacy notice, following the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA).

What we collect — and what we never do

  • No accounts. We never ask for your name, phone number or email to use the app or report gas.
  • Your location stays yours. When you tap “Use my location” or enter a postcode, it is used only to find stations near you. It is never stored with your identity, and never sent anywhere except to show you nearby stations.
  • Reports are anonymous. A report records only the station, the status, the time, and an anonymous technical fingerprint (to stop spam). It cannot identify you.
  • Centre submissions: the centre's public phone is published if approved. Your private contact and your certificate photo are visible only to the moderator, never published.
  • No ads, no tracking for advertising, no selling data.
  • On your device only: your vehicle type (calculator), your favourite stations, and your dark-map choice are stored in your own browser (localStorage). They never leave your phone.

How long we keep things

  • Availability reports: live for 2 hours, then they stop counting. Stored rows are kept for up to 12 months for abuse review, then deleted.
  • Listing reports and confirmations: kept while the issue is open, then for up to 12 months for moderation records.
  • Centre submissions: approved centres stay listed while they are listed. Rejected submissions and their certificate photos are deleted after 6 months. Approved centres can ask for their listing to be removed at any time (see below).
  • Certificates: stored in a private bucket, visible only to the moderator, never published. Deleted when the submission is deleted or the listing is removed.

Ask for removal or correction

If a listing about your centre is wrong, or you want your centre's listing or your submission removed, contact the moderator and say which listing and why. Removal requests are actioned within 7 days.

How live reports work

Anyone can report gas status at a station — no account, no name, no phone number. A report stays live for 2 hours, then the station goes back to “Unconfirmed” so old information never misleads anyone. Reports are public immediately because speed matters at a station; contradicted or abusive reports can be reviewed and hidden by a moderator. Other drivers can confirm a report with one tap (yes / not sure / no).

How verification works

A centre is “Officially accredited” if it appears on the Pi-CNG accredited centres register (checked 18 Nov 2026). Centres that register here are verified by a human — list cross-check, certificate inspection, phone confirmation — before they are listed, and carry an “Independently verified” badge with the verification date. There is no live NADDC/Pi-CNG verification API, so verification is manual for now. Verification badges are reviewed annually; a badge is removed if verification lapses. Entries from 2024 lists carry a “Needs re-verification” badge until re-checked.
